# Bubble visual direction

RGB Bubble should make the desktop feel weightless: dark surfaces sit behind floating translucent panels, while softly lit glass bubbles drift at the edges and leave a calm center for work.

## Visual rules

- Use a midnight blue-black foundation. Keep the dark mode unmistakably dark.
- Red, green, and blue are the main accent family. Keep each hue recognizable rather than blending them into one neon gradient.
- Let a few large forms and small distant bubbles suggest depth and buoyancy. Avoid crowding the wallpaper's center.
- Use borderless translucent fills for Omarchy panels. Keep system panel outlines completely off, and preserve RGB accent borders in application windows and app interfaces.
- Reserve glass translucency for panel surfaces. Keep status text and selected controls high contrast.
- Rounded chat-bubble geometry can vary between pill-like and softly rounded shapes; avoid making every element equally round.

## References

- [Bubble visual guidelines](https://bubble.io/brand) use filled and stroked, rounded bubble forms as part of their visual language. This theme uses generic bubble shapes and is not affiliated with Bubble.
- [Frosted glass UI guide](https://akanoodles.com/thinking/reference/glassmorphism) describes translucent rounded panels, visible background color, and soft light edges as the ingredients of a glass surface.
- [Glassmorphism accessibility and usage](https://webcapitan.com/blog/glassmorphism-ui-design-guide/) recommends keeping text readable, limiting nested glass layers, and reserving transparency for a small number of surfaces.
